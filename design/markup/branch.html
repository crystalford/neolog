The trace: what something leaves behind when it's public, that a machine can follow back to you.

Every public thing on the log has a trace or it doesn't. A trace is four properties — a stable address, a date it was first said, the words behind it, and your name in the file. If it has all four, an engine can cite it and the citation lands on you. If not, it's just text. The trace is the one thing the public side is for. Everything else — formats, feeds, platforms — is how a trace gets carried.

So the question for any thread isn't "should this be published." It's "what trace would this leave, and is it worth leaving?" — and the answer is different for each of the five below.

the fence · settled 4 Sep 2026

Everything above the fence is the product. Everything below it waits until the log has been used daily for a month.

Above the fence — build now

  • Say it once, it exists — an entry, a page, a thing.
  • The log splits a note into its parts and makes what needs making.
  • It looks things up and puts the answer next to your question, labelled.
  • Everything later attaches without asking.
  • Revisions — your correction of your own words, both kept, dated.
  • The trace is shown, per thing, as information: none / a fact / a term / a position / a piece.

Below the fence — designed, not built

  • The offer (page.html §4).
  • Letters, cuts, shorts — the production layer.
  • Drafting anything in your voice.

This is the part that ate the last two attempts: building the tools that were supposed to get to production became the work. So it stays in SPEC.md, fully designed, and doesn't get built until the fence is earned.

What earns it: thirty days of the log in daily use — you said something into it every day and it kept everything. If pages have real material in them by then, the offer is one feature on a working thing. If the log didn't get to thirty days, no production layer would have saved it.
