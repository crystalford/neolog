A page from the spec — one mechanic, shown. The product itself is the log and the expanded entry.

The first take isn't thought out yet. So the log asks three questions before it touches a word — and you answer by talking.

A comedian works a bit by doing it forty times to forty rooms until it lands. You don't have forty nights — you have one first take and no time. So the log plays the room: it finds the claim, finds where it doesn't land, and pushes back three times. You answer by talking. That's the bit worked, in ten minutes instead of a year. Then it hands you the script — read-ready, in your voice, every line traceable to something you said — and you read it and move on.

why the fence moved here, and what the research said

The extractive-only version (cutting ums) solves the wrong problem. You said it: voice-to-text needs restructuring from the ground up, and the real block is that the argument isn't thought out. Two findings settle how to do that without the log becoming the author. First: studies of AI-assisted writing find AI-drafted text comes out polished but structurally hollow — claims asserted without reasons, counterarguments waved at — and the tools that fix it (Prober.ai, Critical Inker) constrain the model to question, never write first, gating any rewrite behind the writer defending their own argument. That's stage 2. Second: the best voice-to-text rewriter (AudioPen) works because it starts from your actual words so rhythm survives, lets you set the degree of rewrite every time on a slider, and trains style on your own past writing. That's stage 3 — and the log has an advantage no app has: your style model is the log itself.

So drafting is now allowed here, under three conditions: you invoked it for this piece; the questions came first; every drafted line is labelled and shown beside yours, and you approve it. This is the operator's call, 6 Sep 2026, and it's recorded in the spec as the one place the drafting fence opens early.
