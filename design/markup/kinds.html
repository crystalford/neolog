Three questions, three existing answers. We don't have to invent any of this.

What kinds of things does a personal site publish? How finished is an idea? How sure is the author? Each has been worked out in public over a decade — by the IndieWeb community, by Gwern's essay site, and by the digital-garden movement that grew out of Mike Caulfield's 2015 talk. Together they answer the questions we kept circling: whether the feed is right, what "ideas" should mean, and what the filters are.

1 · Kinds — what a thing isIndieWeb post types · a decade of personal sites · a W3C Note

The IndieWeb community has published and refined a list of post kinds since 2013. The key idea is that a kind is discovered from what the post has, not chosen from a menu — if it has a photo it's a photo post, if it replies to something it's a reply. That's exactly how the log already works. Their list, mapped to ours:

Verdict on the filters: they were already the right shape — kinds, discovered from the entry. Two things were missing: media wasn't shown (a photo post with no photo isn't one), and ideas had no way to say how finished they were. That's the next table.

2 · Status — how finished an idea isGwern's essay metadata · the digital garden's growth stages

Gwern · per essay, since 2010

Every essay on gwern.net carries: created, modified, a status — "notes" (a pile of links), "draft" (has structure and a thesis), "in progress" (well developed), "finished"; also "abandoned" and "obsolete" — and a confidence word. The status is about the writing's completeness. It's shown at the top of the page, in plain text, and readers use it.

He also has importance, 1–10. We don't take that one. The log doesn't rank what matters; that's your rule.

The garden · seedling → budding → evergreen

Digital gardens mark each note with a growth stage and show it publicly, so a reader knows whether they're reading a fresh thought or a settled one. Same idea as Gwern's status, softer words.

And the brief from 5 September proposed the same ladder for the log: seed → stated → developed → placed. Three sources, one shape. That's the "academic potential" you asked about — not a new page, a status field on an idea, shown clinically.

3 · Confidence — how sureGwern's Kesselman words · our date marks

For a claim · Gwern's list

Gwern tags each essay with one word from the Kesselman List of Estimative Words: certain · highly likely · likely · possible · unlikely · highly unlikely · remote. One word, chosen by the author, shown at the top. Readers on Hacker News called it the best thing about the site — you can be convinced by an argument the author himself marks "possible."

We adopt the list as-is for positions and findings. Terms don't need it; a definition isn't a bet.

For a date · ours, already built

The log already marks how sure a date is: exact · approximate · year only · from memory · looked up. Blurry stays blurry. That's confidence about when; Gwern's is confidence about whether. Both belong on the entry, both in plain words, neither as a number.

4 · The feed itselfCaulfield, 2015 · the garden and the stream

The stream

"A single, time-ordered path with our experience at the center." Twitter, Facebook, every feed. Caulfield's point was that the web collapsed into streams and lost something. But a log is a stream by definition — what happened, when, is the whole point. The public log is a stream and should be. That part of the doubt is settled: for a record, the feed is right.

The garden

"The web as topology… an arrangement and rearrangement of things to one another." Ideas don't live well in a stream — an idea collapses to the day it was said. An idea needs a page: its status, its confidence, its versions, the entries that fed it, what it's linked to. That's the garden, and it's why ideas as a filter felt thin: the filter is the stream view of ideas; each idea's own page is the garden view. Both exist. The glossary is the garden's index.

Settled. The log is the stream; every idea is a garden page; the filters are kinds; status and confidence are two plain words on each idea.

Nothing here is a new lens. It's what personal sites converged on over ten years, adopted rather than invented. What changes on the public log today: media shows inline under seen; the three ideas on the log carry status (draft · stated · finished) and, where it's a bet, how sure. What doesn't change: two nav words, the filters, the unlisted layer. And the one thing we decline from Gwern — importance — because the log never ranks.
