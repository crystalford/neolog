as a log

A thread keeps two things an entry can't: the order things came in, and what each one led from.

A page is a pile — everything about one thing, newest first. A thread is a path — this led to that. The log already has the pieces (every entry is timed to the second inside a note); what it adds is the led-from: each turn points at the turn it came out of. That's one link per step, made when the note is split. Cheap, and it's the difference between a list of five things and the story of a morning.

  • Loops are kept — the second-life page led from where it started cause, not from the step before it.
  • Turns from outside the note are kept — the 10:52 naming came from reading the log, three hours on. It's on the thread.
  • A thread stays open until you say it's done, which you probably never will.
as making

There is no separate creative process. Making something is a thread you keep walking.

There is no separate "creative process" bolted on after the log. The process is this route — extended. Each return is a turn: a revision, a new example, a counter-case, a fork that becomes its own page. The log keeps the growth in order. When there's finally a piece, it's the thread, pruned — and the pruning is a turn too.

  • Wrong turns are kept. The dead ends are part of how you got there.
  • Nothing is drafted for you above the fence. The thread is yours; the log keeps it in order and shows you the shape.
  • The shape is the offer, later: not "there's a piece here" but "here's the route you've walked — and here's the turn you haven't taken."
