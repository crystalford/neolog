What is Neural Engine Optimization, and how is it different from the other acronyms?
Neural Engine Optimization is my name for making something worth citing, as opposed to making something easy to find. The other acronyms — search, answer and generative optimization — all improve how easily an existing fact is found. Neural Engine Optimization is about being the source of a combination nobody else could have produced.

Search optimization, answer optimization and generative optimization converge on the same advice: be clearly named, be described consistently, be dated, be quotable. That advice is genuinely good and it is entirely about retrieval mechanics. It explains how to be found. It does not explain what to have that is worth finding. My definition of Neural Engine Optimization exists to name that gap.

The gap matters more now that most published material is synthesis of other published material. What's scarce isn't well-structured content, it's an original combination — and an original combination usually requires a primary source. A person with twenty years of their own life in the log has that; a content team doesn't. That asymmetry is the whole idea.

How is Neural Engine Optimization different from Generative Engine Optimization?

Generative Engine Optimization makes existing content easier for an AI to retrieve and cite — structure, dates, consistency. Neural Engine Optimization is about whether the content contains anything a synthesis engine hasn't already seen a hundred times. GEO is formatting; NEO is originality.

Why can a person do Neural Engine Optimization when a company can't?

Because original combination needs a primary source, and a person with twenty years of their own life in the log has one. A content team synthesizes other people's material; the log is first-hand by construction.

Does Neural Engine Optimization replace SEO?

No. Neural Engine Optimization sits on top of it. Search optimization still decides whether a page is found; NEO decides whether, once found, the page has anything worth quoting. Both are needed and they don't compete.

What is reduction in neolog?
Reduction is how neolog stores things: every entry is one short line, with the full story kept behind it. You can read a life as a single sentence, a paragraph, or the whole record — a tagline, a line, or every word. It runs both directions, and the expansion always comes from the person, never from the system.

The idea came from not wanting to scroll. A new job is one line of news; behind that line is a year without work, a move, the job I applied for before it. I wanted the line on top and the rest reachable, not the rest in the way. What makes it more than a summary is that every layer keeps the way back down — the line cites the beats, the beats cite the exact words, the words carry an ID.

The constraint that keeps it honest is that when the system expands something, it does so by asking me, not by filling in. Interpretation is allowed, but it sits in a separate, labelled layer and never gets blended with what I actually said.

smaller questions inside this one

Is reduction the same as summarising?

No. A summary replaces the source; reduction indexes it. Every reduced layer in neolog points at the exact words it came from, and you can always go down.

Is reduction lossless?

No, and it shouldn't be. Each level of detail loses something on purpose — a tagline is twelve words. What it never loses is the way back. The honest word is reversible.

How does reduction work with a machine reading a long transcript?

The transcript is split into the actual words with stable IDs. Summaries are built in layers on top, each citing the spans below. When the system needs to answer anything, it walks down to the spans and answers from those — the tree is for finding, not knowing. The full design is in the pipeline spec.
