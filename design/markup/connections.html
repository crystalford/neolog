Every way something gets into the log. Eight by hand, from wherever you are. Seven on their own, each a switch.

Two kinds of way in. By hand, there are eight doors — the box, the phone, your earbuds, the share sheet, a browser button, an email address, a text number, an AI session — and every one leads to the same box and ends in the same one-line receipt. On their own, seven sources can add things without you: each is a switch you turned on after seeing the first ten things it would take, each shows what it has brought in, and turning one off stops it that second without touching what already came.

Turning one on

You pick the source and the account. Before anything lands on the log, it shows you the first ten things it would take — real ones, from your data — and how each would read as a line. You say yes to the source, not to each item. From then on it runs.

What it never does

It doesn't read the body of an email, the description of a calendar event, or the content of a file — only the parts that say when something happened and what it was. It doesn't recognise faces. It doesn't name places you haven't named. It doesn't decide anything is important. It sorts by time, attaches what matches a day or a thing you've already made, and stops.

Turning one offthe switch controls the future, never the past

Off means nothing new from here. It never means "remove what came."

What a source already brought in stays on the log, because those things happened and you were there. If you want them gone you bury them, one at a time or a whole source's worth — and buried is a state, not a deletion. Turning a source back on later picks up from the day it stopped, and tells you how many days it missed. The gap is shown, not filled.

Eight doors, one receipt.

However something gets in — typed, spoken, shared, forwarded, texted — the same thing happens: dated, filed, one line back you can undo. No door has its own rules.

Nothing records silently.

Every source was switched on by you, here, after seeing the first ten things it would take. There is no default-on. There is no "improve your experience" toggle buried in settings.

Each source shows what it brought.

A count, the most recent thing, and a link to it — on this page, always. If a source is adding things you don't want, you can see that here before you see it on the log.

Off is forward-only.

Turning a source off stops it that second. What's already on the log stays, because it happened. Removing the past is burial, done by hand, and marked.
