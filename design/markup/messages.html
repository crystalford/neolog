Texts, emails, messages. The only thing on your log that's half someone else's.

Everything else here is yours to keep and yours to publish. A conversation isn't — half the words belong to the other person. So this kind works differently from every other: your side is yours; their side is kept, attached to them, and never crosses to public on your say-so alone. Not around their privacy. With it.

Your side — yours

Each of your messages is an entry you "said", dated to the minute, same as anything you type into the log. They can become key sentences, go to pages, go public — normal rules.

Their side — theirs

Kept, in full, in the thread and on Leif's page. Not an entry on your log's front page. Not searchable as "things that happened" — searchable as "things Leif said." Never public without his yes. A fact he states is filed as Leif says, not as fact.

2their yes

Four states for another person's words. You set them per person, and the default is the most private one.

This is the whole design. Not a checkbox you tick once — a state per person, changeable, shown on their page, and enforced everywhere their words appear.

3where it comes from

Forwarded by you, always. The log never reads your inbox.

Messages arrive one way: you send them. Forward an email, share a thread from the messages app, paste a chat. The log doesn't connect to your accounts and pull conversations — the email door is for you writing to the log, not for the log reading what others wrote to you. Correspondence is chosen, one thread at a time, because every thread has someone else in it.

Two owners, one entry.

Your side is yours — entries, quotes, public, normal rules. Their side is theirs — kept, attached to them, filed as "they said," never public on your word alone.

Their yes is a fact on the log.

Not a checkbox. When they said it, how, for which words. Revocable, and revoking pulls the quotes. The default, for everyone, is the most private state.
