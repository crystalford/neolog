Five acronyms describe how to get found. None describes how to be worth finding.

What I learned in an afternoon about SEO, AEO, GEO and LLMO — and why a personal log already does most of it without trying, except the part that matters.

¹ Search Engine Land² OrganiKPI³ MaxAEO⁴ Ahrefs⁵ entry 1,858

I spent an afternoon reading about how AI search engines decide what to cite. There are, at last count, five acronyms for this: SEO, AEO, GEO, LLMO, and AIO.1 The advice under all five is roughly the same: be findable, be consistent, be quotable. Name yourself the same way everywhere. Answer questions in short sentences. Put dates on things.2

What struck me is that a personal log already does all of that, by accident. Every entry has a date because that's what a log is. Every fact has a source because it's my own words on tape. The name is consistent because it's one person. I didn't build any of this for engines — I built it because I didn't have a record of my own life — and it turns out that a record is exactly what they were looking for.5

What the five have in common

All five are about retrieval. They assume the fact already exists somewhere and the job is to be the copy that gets picked. SEO gets you ranked as a link. AEO gets you lifted as the answer. GEO gets you cited inside a paragraph the engine wrote.3 LLMO is the plumbing underneath — how a model resolves you as one entity rather than three.4 Different surfaces, same question: of all the places this is said, which one do we quote?

"It's like we went with a more vercelly sort of sleek — not the yellow amber."Not this one. This is what I actually sound like when I'm not being quoted. Kept for honesty.

None of them asks the prior question: is there anything here worth retrieving in the first place? Most of what's published now is a restatement of something else. Engines have noticed — the post-model web is flooded with synthesis, and what's scarce is a genuinely new angle nobody else has run.3

The sixth word

So I gave that part a name. Neural Engine Optimization — NEO — is optimising to be the source of something new a synthesis engine wants to cite, rather than optimising to be retrieved faster. It's the axis the other five can't cover, because it isn't about formatting. It's about having said something first.

The name came before the strategy did — neolog was named for neologism, years ago. Then the acronym war happened to rhyme with it. I asked whether that was just funny or actually the point, and I think it's the point: a personal log is the one kind of publication that can run NEO, because it requires an actual life to synthesize from, and a content team doesn't have one.5

What I'll do differently

Not much, on the log itself. The dates, names and sources were already there. What changes is what I let out: not posts, but terms I've coined, positions I hold, things I've counted — each with the day I first said it and the words I said it in. Short things a machine can lift, sitting on top of long things a person can read. This letter is one of the long things.
