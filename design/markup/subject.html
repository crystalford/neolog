a note on the technology · is it good enough for this? · 4 Sep 2026 · reasoned, not sourced

Mostly yes, for the parts that matter — with one part that will be weak for a while.

Reliable now

Making a page from one mention. Deciding "this is a thought about something, not an event" and naming it is a one-shot classification a current model does well. The failure mode is over-eager naming — which is why the receipt has not a page, just a thought.

Attaching later mentions. Matching new text against a small list of your own pages is retrieval, not reasoning. It's the same mechanic as attaching a photo to a day. Very reliable; the risk is false attaches, which are one tap to undo.

The one-paragraph version. Summarising nine entries you wrote into one paragraph is exactly what these models are for. The rule that keeps it honest: every sentence must trace to an entry, and the paragraph is always editable.

Less reliable — design around it

Knowing when there's "genuinely enough." The model can't judge ripeness well from the outside. What it can do is check for structure: is there a claim, a mechanism, examples, a counter-example? If yes, offer. That's a checklist, not taste — and it's why the offer shows its work instead of just saying "there's a piece here."

Finding the hole. Noticing that Sinatra is mentioned twice as a problem and never resolved is real reasoning across entries. Current models do this inconsistently — good on a short page, worse on a long one. The mitigation is the same as everywhere: run it twice, only surface what both passes agree on.

The draft itself. Assembling your words into an argument in order is fine. Writing the joins in your voice is where it will be visibly mediocre for a while. The draft should be mostly your sentences with the joins marked as the log's, so you can see exactly what to rewrite.

Verdict: build the page, the attaching, and the paragraph now — they'll work. Build the offer as a structural checklist with the hole shown, not as a judgment of quality. Expect the drafting to be the weak link for a year or two, and design so that its weakness is visible rather than hidden.
