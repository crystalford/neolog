Most of what AI engines cite is other people's pages. So this site has to show up there too, not just here.

Everything on the log, the about page and the questions makes my own pages easy to cite. But a study by Muck Rack found that about 84% of the sources AI engines cite are earned — someone else writing about you — not pages you own. This page is about that other 84%: where those citations actually come from, and what I can do there without turning into a marketer.

the opening
person > brand

Engines are shifting from brand entities to named individuals — a person can be traced, has accountable expertise, offers experience over marketing copy. A logged life is exactly that. A content team isn't.

Where citations actually come from

Fifteen websites receive two-thirds of everything AI engines cite. Four of them are places an ordinary person can post.

That's from six studies covering 680 million citations between 2024 and 2026. The concentration is more extreme than Google's old ranking ever was — and the top of the list isn't news publishers. It's discussion communities and video.

Reddit
#1 · ~40% multi-engine · 47% on Perplexity

Upvotes are human consensus at scale. Subreddits are topical. URLs are durable. It's the closest thing on the web to what an answer engine wants — and the unit is the subreddit, not the platform.

Three moves, none posted without you

The log notices when someone, somewhere, is asking a question I've already answered — and drafts a reply from what I said. I decide whether to post it.

This is the same process that turns a recording into entries, pointed outward. I don't write for Reddit. I said something in the car; the log finds the thread.

01Answer where it's askedreddit · forumswatches 6 subreddits

The single most practical tactic in the research: find the threads already showing up in AI answers, and be in them. Not a new post — a substantive answer in an existing conversation that engines already trust.

The log watches subreddits that match your subjects. When a question appears that your record can answer first-hand, with a dated receipt, it drafts the reply from your own words and links back to the entry. Short promotional replies get downvoted and removed; a specific answer with a real failure in it doesn't.

What makes this honest: the answer already exists in the log. The draft is a retrieval, not an invention.

02Ship the tapeyoutube~400 recordings · 0 published

YouTube is the second most-cited domain in AI answers — and you have roughly four hundred first-hand recordings that nobody can see. This was always the weakest part of the product. It's now the second-largest citation channel on earth, which is a different reason to fix it.

The log already turns a recording into five shapes. Each cut gets a question-shaped title, a transcript, pages that match the sub-questions, and a description carrying the canonical sentence. The video is the receipt; the transcript is the passage.

03Become a thingwikidata · sameAsone-time · 20 minutes

An engine encountering "Chris Telford" in a Reddit thread and "Chris Telford" on the facts page has no way to know they're the same person — unless there's a stable identifier both point to. That's a Wikidata QID. It needs no notability, and it's the anchor every other signal completes from.

Once it exists, the facts page declares it in sameAs, LinkedIn's bio links to it, and the log's check on names in stage four gets a ground truth instead of a best guess. This is the least glamorous item on the page and the one the research puts second in priority, right after the entity home you already have.

Numbers other people can use

what this can't promise

Third-party channels are volatile in a way your own pages aren't. ChatGPT's Reddit citation rate swung from roughly 60% of responses to roughly 10% inside a single year. The people doing this well treat third-party platforms as a supplement, never the plan — the plan is still the entity home, the facts, the questions answered on your own ground.

And nothing here manufactures interest. The log can find the thread, draft the answer and cut the tape. Whether the answer is worth upvoting is decided by whether you actually had something first-hand to say — which is the only part of this the log can't do for you, and the only part that was ever the product.
