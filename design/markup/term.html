I spent an afternoon reading how AI search engines decide who to cite, and realised the name of this site already had the answer in it.
a term · draftpublic
There are five acronyms for getting cited by AI — SEO, AEO, GEO, LLMO, AIO. They all end in EO. I had never thought of neolog as something to be found; it was a record, for me. Then I looked at the name: NEO-log. That was the moment two ideas merged. The five acronyms are about being found faster. None is about being worth finding. I gave that missing piece a name: Neural Engine Optimization. I'm not sure about "Neural" — the idea is settled, the word isn't.

SEO, AEO, GEO and LLMO are four names for the same job: making something that already exists easier to retrieve. Structure it, mark it up, keep it fresh, phrase it so a machine can lift it cleanly. All four assume the thing is worth retrieving and work on the getting-found half.

None of them is about being worth citing in the first place. That half needs its own name because the strategies pull opposite ways: retrieval rewards conforming to a pattern, and being cited as a source rewards saying something nobody else has said. A synthesis engine has no reason to quote a restatement of what it already knows — but every reason to quote a coined term, an original count, or a first-hand account, because those exist in exactly one place. Neural Engine Optimization is that half: being the source rather than the summary. It is also why the name works — neolog already carried it.
